# Writing the `{{服装设计}}` Block

Use this reference when creating or revising an original outfit.

## Required Content

Write one self-contained design block that fixes:

- look name and overall intent;
- intended character body proportions and coverage;
- every garment piece and its silhouette;
- front, back, left, and right structural relationships;
- layer order and wearing method;
- real fabrics, hard materials, lining, and surface finish;
- seams, darts, hems, zippers, clasps, straps, boning, hinges, or other construction;
- attachment and weight-bearing points for rigid or projecting elements;
- footwear and each accessory, including exact quantity and side;
- a final list of view-invariant counts and positions;
- prohibited substitutions that would collapse the intended design.

The block may be detailed. Do not move this detail into the universal template.

Keep biological styling out of the air-mannequin garment block. Hats, veils, hair ornaments, earrings, glasses, and other wearable objects belong here; skin makeup, eyebrows, lashes, lips, hair texture, and the character's actual hairstyle belong to the later character-styling stage.

## Convert Concepts into Physical Decisions

Translate abstract style words before rendering:

- glow → reflective thread, electroluminescent fiber, sequins, or concealed practical lighting;
- crystal → cut acrylic, transparent resin, glass beads, or mineral-like plates with visible mounts;
- levitation → stiff organza, transparent support line, boning, cantilevered frame, or optical layering;
- biomorphic → shaped pattern pieces, pleating, laser-cut petals, articulated scales, or organic seam paths;
- liquid → coated textile, patent leather, laminated film, glossy satin, or controlled drape.

State how every unusual element is fabricated and attached. If it cannot be cut, sewn, molded, mounted, tied, zipped, buckled, or otherwise supported, redesign it before generation.

## Keep the Design Coherent

Complexity is allowed, but organize it around:

- one dominant motif;
- one primary silhouette action;
- one main material contrast;
- a restrained accessory set.

Avoid accumulating unrelated futuristic and fantasy clichés. Describe the actual garment instead of describing an atmosphere around it.

## Improve Four-view Consistency

Repeated elements need exact identity anchors. State:

- fixed quantity;
- size order;
- starting and ending body landmarks;
- attachment seam or hardware;
- which elements remain visible from each side.

Prefer a few named structural elements over many interchangeable decorations. If a result repeatedly changes counts across views, simplify the repeated structure inside the design block rather than lengthening the universal template.
