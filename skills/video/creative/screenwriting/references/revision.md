# Revision without scope drift

1. Read the current approved draft and requested notes. Distinguish a local line edit, scene repair, structural revision and factual correction.
2. Identify the intended improvement and the smallest set of scenes that depends on it. Preserve tone and unresolved story choices outside the request.
3. Explain material tradeoffs only when a requested change breaks established causality, runtime or production feasibility. Offer a concrete repair, not a generic objection.
4. Revise with stable scene IDs and a new draft version. List the exact character-state, dialogue, action and duration changes needed downstream.
5. Re-read the affected sequence plus its entry and exit. Check pronouns, referents, information known by characters, prop transfers and whether a payoff still has its setup.

```yaml
draft: 4
based_on: 3
requested_change: Make the confession indirect.
changed_scenes: [S03]
preserved: [key transfer, Jin learns Maya returned, tin stays closed]
downstream_recheck: [S03-board, S03-voice, S03-edit]
reason: Dialogue changed; story exit state and later scenes remain valid.
timing: Read-through not yet repeated.
```

For factual revisions, check every repeated occurrence and visual implication, not just the narration sentence. For a story rewrite, distinguish changing canonical truth from delaying the audience's knowledge of that truth. Do not label a review independent if the writer performed it in the same role execution.
