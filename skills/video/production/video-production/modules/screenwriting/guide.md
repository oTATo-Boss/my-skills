# Screenwriting

Write for what an audience can see, hear and infer over time. Preserve the user's premise, approved canon, factual sources and requested writing stage. Use the existing project format and folders. This skill does not generate footage or convert the project into a novel-writing system.

## Choose the writing task

- **Story/outline only:** explore the causal structure, characters or scene order at that level. Do not produce a full script until the request includes it.
- **Dramatic short/film:** track a character's goal, resistance, choice and consequence. Fit the dramatic scope to runtime and available settings/performers.
- **Series:** distinguish season/series promise, episode movement and scene purpose. Maintain information release and state between episodes; each episode should change something beyond ending on a cliffhanger.
- **Explainer/documentary:** organize questions, evidence, causal explanation and earned conclusions. Separate sourced facts, interpretation, reconstructions and fictional illustrations.
- **MV:** first map the supplied music's sections. Narrative MVs need character/state causality; visual MVs can develop a motif without inventing a plot. Avoid turning every lyric into a literal shot.

For the selected mode, read [formats.md](references/formats.md), which includes scene, audiovisual and series templates. Read [revision.md](references/revision.md) when reviewing an existing script or responding to notes.

## Draft from the available evidence

1. State the intended audience experience and dramatic or explanatory question. Inventory material already approved and facts that must be researched. Do not invent real product claims, quotations, history or scientific evidence.
2. Outline beats as changes in knowledge, intention, relationship, stakes or understanding. Each scene/section earns its place through a change; descriptive atmosphere can support that change without carrying the whole structure.
3. Assign observable action and sound. Replace unfilmable interior explanation with behavior, dialogue, image, editing or expressly chosen voiceover. Do not prescribe every camera move unless it changes meaning or the task requests shooting-script detail.
4. Give dialogue intention, subtext and distinct voice. Read it aloud or time a representative performance when possible. Do not assume a fixed words-per-minute value proves runtime.
5. Mark source-dependent and production-dependent beats. An effect requiring a simulation, a real location or a licensed song should be visible in the handoff before production begins.
6. Estimate duration by section and action. Validate timing with read-through/animatic/actual narration before locking a timeline. Long dialogue, silence, physical action and music each need their own timing judgment.

## Handoff to production

Deliver the requested stage plus a short change note when revising. Keep scene IDs stable when the underlying scene survives. New inserted scenes receive new IDs; do not silently renumber downstream shot references.

Include the facts and continuity production must preserve: story time, location, present characters, props and their state, information known by each character, planned sound, unresolved research and the next intended stage. Narrative truth and production order are distinct.

Review for causal clarity, agency, legibility without explanatory notes, duration, source support and feasibility at the chosen production scale. A script is not approved merely because all fields are filled. Return specific weaknesses with the affected scene and intended repair.

Do not enforce one genre formula, mandatory opening shock, universal three-act percentages or social-platform hook cadence. Choose structure for this film and audience.
